## What works

- Numbered ticket steps, two directions and three target distances make the task approachable; optional comparison provides depth.
- Shared color tokens, company glyphs, button primitives and reserved numeric space provide an existing design-system foundation. Preserve these while refining state consistency.
- Pending/checking/retry feedback respects authoritative trade state. The premium-versus-payout example and tailored daily lessons can teach the central misconception.

## Priority issues

### [P1] Explain consequences beside Buy

Evidence: src/screens/ticket/TicketBuilder.tsx:63 shows quantity and Price limit; :239 places that summary above an optional scenario; :248 shows total cost on Buy. Break-even is elsewhere on the chart and becomes explicit in the owned-ticket summary.

Why: a beginner can confuse crossing a target with making a profit and may not understand that the whole quoted cost can be lost.

Fix: keep server-authoritative break-even and potential full loss visible beside the quoted cost. Explain Price limit without conflating maximum permitted price with confirmed execution. Retain optional scenarios.

### [P1] Separate financial outcome from evidence of skill

Evidence: src/screens/words.ts:50 uses Grow it or blow it; :105 assigns ranks solely from ending cash, including a claim that a profitable player read news well. src/screens/FinalScreen.tsx:64 foregrounds rank and wealth.

Fix: preserve the game's playful character and money result, but lead with completion and a concrete tradeoff to review. Avoid inferring competence from ending cash.

## Cognitive load

Four source-based checklist concerns: competing live regions, multiple simultaneous decisions, more than four choices at some decision points, and memory demands when context disappears. Grouping, numbered stages and optional detail provide useful mitigation. Actual viewport density remains unverified.

Choice sets: six companies; seven comparison company filters including All; five budget routes including Custom; five final review days. These counts flag decisions to inspect, not a rule to remove necessary companies or split the five-day review arbitrarily.

## Emotional journey and personas

The start builds playful confidence, a trade creates anticipation, and loss or disconnection is the vulnerable moment. Daily explanations support understanding. A cash-based rank currently has the strongest final word.

- First-time learner: can reach Buy without a direct statement of break-even and full potential loss in its summary.
- Interrupted mobile learner: loses source-rating context in comparison and the tailored lesson when revisiting a day. Short-height styles hide value explanations.
- Keyboard/screen-reader user: native dialogs, explicit focus styles, chart arrow-key support and live notices are positive source evidence. Timed transitions and actual focus/announcement behavior still require browser testing.

## Design system and micro-interactions

Shared tokens and primitives already exist in src/styles.css and src/screens/ui.tsx. Document and extend them incrementally.

A focused interaction pass should distinguish pressed, selected, pending, confirmed, rejected and stale states. Phase actions currently disable while busy but retain their original label (src/screens/desk/PhaseActions.tsx:23). Give those actions specific progress feedback; reserve completion feedback for authoritative confirmation.

Comparison changes should preserve context and orientation. Keep live numeric values spatially stable. The real/hope bar already transitions over 200ms; do not add animation to every price tick. Check interruption, repeated activation, keyboard behavior and reduced motion in a working browser before judging motion quality.
